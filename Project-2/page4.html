Imperfect Cinema

The emergence of poor images reminds one of a classic Third Cinema manifesto, “For an Imperfect Cinema,” by Julio García Espinosa, written in Cuba in the late 1960s.

Espinosa argues for an imperfect cinema because, in his words, “perfect cinema—technically and artistically masterful-is almost always reactionary cinema.”

The imperfect cinema is one that strives to overcome the divisions of labor within class society. It merges art with life and science, blurring the distinction between consumer and producer, audience and author.

It insists upon its own imperfection, is popular but not consumerist, committed without becoming bureaucratic.
